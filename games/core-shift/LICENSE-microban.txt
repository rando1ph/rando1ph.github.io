Core Shift — level attribution
==============================

The campaign's puzzles come from Microban, a set of 155 hand-made Sokoban
puzzles by David W. Skinner (2000).

  Author : David W. Skinner  <sasquatch@bentonrea.com>
  Set    : Microban (155 puzzles, released April 2000)
  Source : http://www.sneezingtiger.com/sokoban/levels/microbanText.html
           https://github.com/sebnozzi/minimicro-sokoban (microban.txt)

-------

The author's own terms, quoted from his level page:

  "My sets are available here in text format. ... These sets may be
   freely distributed provided they remain properly credited."

Microban is therefore redistributed here free of charge, with the author
credited in the game's credits line, in the header comment of levels.js
and in this file. No Microban puzzle is original to randolf.dev and none
is presented as such. Only the presentation — the spacecraft maintenance

Microban is a fan-made level set for Sokoban. Sokoban itself was created
by Hiroyuki Imabayashi and is a trademark of Thinking Rabbit; no Thinking
Rabbit levels are used anywhere in this game.

Campaign selection
------------------

The game ships 103 of the 155 puzzles, kept in Microban's original order.
The rest were filtered out by three rules, each in the service of the
ship's constraints rather than of the levels themselves:

  · boards with either side longer than 11 cells are dropped — a core
    would stop being a comfortable touch target on a 390px phone;
  · boards the bundled push-state solver cannot prove solvable within
    500k states are dropped — a level is never shipped unverified;
  · boards whose initial-state solve takes more than ~0.4s on the build
    machine are dropped — the in-game Hint runs the same search, so every
    shipped level stays Hint-fast. Measured slow: Microban 78, 114, 133.

levels.js records the original Microban number for every entry
(`source: "Microban N"`); `id` is the in-game campaign number (1..103).

Verification
------------

Every shipped puzzle is verified solvable by validate.js, and each stored
solution is replayed through the real engine before the level is accepted.
